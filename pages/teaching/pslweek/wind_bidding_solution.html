Day-ahead bidding of a wind farm

François Pacaud
November 2026

Motivation

This project is a sequel to the tutorial on the optimal management of an energy storage. It uses the same tools: the language Julia, the modeler JuMP and the solver HiGHS.

] add Plots, JuMP, HiGHS

A wind producer sells its energy on the day-ahead market. Every day before noon, it must commit to the quantity of energy it will deliver during each hour of the following day. At that time, the wind production is not known: it is only forecast. The next day, any deviation between the committed quantity and the actual production is settled on the balancing market at an unfavorable price:

  • If the producer delivers more than committed (surplus), the extra energy is paid less than the day-ahead price.

  • If the producer delivers less than committed (deficit), the missing energy is charged more than the day-ahead price.

This is a two-stage decision problem:

  1. First stage (here-and-now): choose the day-ahead bids, before the wind is known.

  2. Second stage (wait-and-see): observe the wind and settle the imbalances.

Wind production

We consider a wind farm with capacity $\overline{W} = 10$ MW. The production depends on the wind speed $v$ through the power curve of the turbines:

farm = WindFarm(10.0)
plot(0:0.1:30, v -> power_curve(farm, v), lw=2.0, color=:black, label="")
xlabel!("Wind speed [m/s]")
ylabel!("Power [MW]")

Question 1. Comment the power curve. What are the cut-in, rated and cut-out speeds? Why does the production drop to zero above 25 m/s?

Solution. Below the cut-in speed (3 m/s) the turbines do not turn. Between 3 and 12 m/s the power grows like the cube of the wind speed (the kinetic energy flux is proportional to $v^3$). Above the rated speed (12 m/s) the blades are pitched to keep the power at its nominal value. Above the cut-out speed (25 m/s) the turbines are stopped to protect them from mechanical damage.

The forecaster provides a forecast $\overline{v}_t$ of the wind speed. The realized speed is $v_t = \overline{v}_t + ε_t$, where the forecast error $ε_t$ follows an auto-regressive process:

\[ ε_t = ρ \, ε_{t-1} + σ \, η_t \; , \quad η_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \; , \quad ε_0 = 0 \; . \]

The realized production is $w_t = φ(v_t)$, with $φ$ the power curve. We sample scenarios of the wind production with the function generate_wind_scenarios:

v_forecast = wind_speed_forecast(T)
w_forecast = power_curve.(Ref(farm), v_forecast)

Question 2. Comment the scenarios. How does the spread evolve with the lead time? Why? Compare the forecast production $φ(\overline{v}_t)$ with the average production $\mathbb{E}[φ(v_t)]$ (estimate it on 10,000 scenarios). Explain the difference.

Solution. The variance of $ε_t$ is $σ^2 (1 - ρ^{2t}) / (1 - ρ^2)$: it is zero at the gate closure and grows with the lead time, converging to $σ^2 / (1 - ρ^2)$. The spread is also smaller when the production is close to $0$ or $\overline{W}$, where the power curve is flat.

wind_large = generate_wind_scenarios(farm, v_forecast, 10_000; seed=2)

plot(w_forecast, lw=2.0, color=:darkblue, label="φ(E[v])")

xlabel!("Hours")

The power curve is nonlinear: it is convex at low speed and concave near the rated speed. By Jensen's inequality, $\mathbb{E}[φ(v)] ≥ φ(\mathbb{E}[v])$ where $φ$ is convex, and $\mathbb{E}[φ(v)] ≤ φ(\mathbb{E}[v])$ where $φ$ is concave. Forecasting the speed is not the same as forecasting the power.

Part I: Deterministic bidding

We denote by $x_t ∈ [0, \overline{W}]$ the energy bid on the day-ahead market for hour $t$. Given a realization $w_t$ of the wind, the producer delivers $y_t ≤ w_t$ ($w_t - y_t$ is curtailed) and the revenue for hour $t$ is

\[ λ_t x_t + λ^+_t \, (y_t - x_t)^+ - λ^-_t \, (x_t - y_t)^+ \; . \]

As the prices are positive, it is never profitable to curtail: we can set $y_t = w_t$. The following function evaluates the revenue of a bid $x$ on a set of scenarios:

"""
    evaluate_bid(x, λ, λp, λm, wind)

Return the revenue [€] of the bid `x` on each scenario of `wind`
(matrix of dimension `(T, n_scenarios)`).
"""
function evaluate_bid(x::Vector, λ::Vector, λp::Vector, λm::Vector, wind::Matrix)
    T, nscen = size(wind)
    revenue = zeros(nscen)
    for k in 1:nscen, t in 1:T
        surplus = max(wind[t, k] - x[t], 0.0)
        deficit = max(x[t] - wind[t, k], 0.0)
        revenue[k] += λ[t] * x[t] + λp[t] * surplus - λm[t] * deficit
    end
    return revenue
end

Question 3. Suppose the producer knows the future wind production $w$. What is the optimal bid? What is the corresponding revenue?

Solution. With perfect information, the optimal bid is $x_t = w_t$: as $λ^+_t < λ_t < λ^-_t$, any imbalance loses money. The revenue is then $∑_t λ_t w_t$.

Part II: Two-stage stochastic program

We approximate the uncertainty by $N$ scenarios $w^1, ⋯, w^N$, each with probability $1/N$ (Sample Average Approximation, SAA). We introduce the second-stage variables

  • $Δ^+_{t,k} ≥ 0$: surplus at hour $t$ in scenario $k$ [MWh]

  • $Δ^-_{t,k} ≥ 0$: deficit at hour $t$ in scenario $k$ [MWh]

  • $0 ≤ y_{t,k} ≤ w^k_t$: delivered energy at hour $t$ in scenario $k$ [MWh]

The two-stage stochastic program writes

\[ \begin{aligned} \max_{x, Δ^+, Δ^-, y} \; & ∑_{t=1}^T λ_t x_t + \frac{1}{N} ∑_{k=1}^N ∑_{t=1}^T \Big( λ^+_t Δ^+_{t,k} - λ^-_t Δ^-_{t,k} \Big) \\ \text{s.t.}\quad & y_{t,k} - x_t = Δ^+_{t,k} - Δ^-_{t,k} \\ & 0 ≤ x_t ≤ \overline{W} \; , \quad 0 ≤ y_{t,k} ≤ w^k_t \; , \quad Δ^+_{t,k}, Δ^-_{t,k} ≥ 0 \end{aligned} \]

Question 5. Which variables are the first-stage variables? The second-stage variables? Write the second-stage value function $Q(x, w)$. Show that $Q$ is concave in $x$.

Solution. The bid $x$ is decided before the wind is known (first stage). The surplus, deficit and delivered energy depend on the scenario (second stage). The value function is

\[ Q(x, w) = \max_{y, Δ^±} \Big\{ ∑_t λ^+_t Δ^+_t - λ^-_t Δ^-_t \;:\; y_t - x_t = Δ^+_t - Δ^-_t,\; 0 ≤ y ≤ w,\; Δ^± ≥ 0 \Big\} \]

This is the value of a parametric LP where $x$ only appears in the right-hand side of the constraints. By LP duality, $Q(\cdot, w)$ is the minimum of finitely many affine functions of $x$: it is concave and polyhedral. Here, $Q(x, w) = ∑_t λ^+_t (w_t - x_t)^+ - λ^-_t (x_t - w_t)^+$.

We implement the problem with JuMP:

using JuMP, HiGHS

"""
    build_two_stage_model(farm, λ, λp, λm, wind)

Build the SAA of the two-stage bidding problem on the scenarios `wind`
(matrix of dimension `(T, n_scenarios)`).
"""
function build_two_stage_model(farm::WindFarm, λ, λp, λm, wind::Matrix)
    T, N = size(wind)
    model = Model()
    # First stage
    @variable(model, 0.0 <= x[1:T] <= farm.Wmax)
    # Second stage
    @variable(model, 0.0 <= y[t=1:T, k=1:N] <= wind[t, k])
    @variable(model, Δp[1:T, 1:N] >= 0.0)
    @variable(model, Δm[1:T, 1:N] >= 0.0)
    @constraint(model, balance[t=1:T, k=1:N], y[t, k] - x[t] == Δp[t, k] - Δm[t, k])
    @objective(
        model,
        Max,
        sum(λ[t] * x[t] for t in 1:T) +
        sum(λp[t] * Δp[t, k] - λm[t] * Δm[t, k] for t in 1:T, k in 1:N) / N
    )
    return model
end

Part III: Explicit solution

Without storage, the objective and the constraints separate hour by hour. For each hour $t$ we have to solve a one-dimensional problem:

\[ \max_{0 ≤ x_t ≤ \overline{W}} \; λ_t x_t + \mathbb{E}\Big[ λ^+_t (w_t - x_t)^+ - λ^-_t (x_t - w_t)^+ \Big] \]

This is the well-known newsvendor problem.

Question 8. Let $F_t$ be the cumulative distribution function of $w_t$. Show that an optimal bid satisfies

\[ F_t(x_t^\star) = \frac{λ_t - λ^+_t}{λ^-_t - λ^+_t} \; , \]

that is, $x^\star_t$ is a quantile of the wind production. What is this quantile here?

Solution. Let $f(x) = λ_t x + \mathbb{E}[λ^+_t (w_t - x)^+ - λ^-_t (x - w_t)^+]$. The function $f$ is concave and its (super)derivative is

\[ f'(x) = λ_t - λ^+_t \, \mathbb{P}[w_t > x] - λ^-_t \, \mathbb{P}[w_t ≤ x] = (λ_t - λ^+_t) - (λ^-_t - λ^+_t) F_t(x) \; . \]

Setting $f'(x) = 0$ gives the result. With $λ^±_t = α^± λ_t$, the quantile $(1 - α^+) / (α^- - α^+) = 0.4$ does not depend on $t$.

Question 9. For the empirical distribution of the $N$ scenarios, the quantile is $x_t = w^{(⌈qN⌉)}_t$, the $⌈qN⌉$-th smallest value among $w^1_t, ⋯, w^N_t$. Implement this formula and check that it gives the same optimal value as the LP. Is the solution of the LP unique?

Solution.

function newsvendor_bid(λ, λp, λm, wind::Matrix)
    T, N = size(wind)
    x = zeros(T)
    for t in 1:T
        q = (λ[t] - λp[t]) / (λm[t] - λp[t])
        x[t] = sort(wind[t, :])[ceil(Int, q * N)]
    end
    return x
end

The two values coincide. The solution is not unique when $qN$ is an integer: any bid between the $qN$-th and the $(qN+1)$-th smallest values is optimal (here $qN = 40$). The bid is also not unique at hours where several scenarios give $0$ or $\overline{W}$.

Question 10. How does the optimal quantile evolve when $α^- → 1$? When $α^+ → 0$? Interpret.

Solution. With $q = (1 - α^+) / (α^- - α^+)$: when $α^- → 1$ the deficit costs the day-ahead price, so committing is free: $q → 1$ and the producer bids the maximum plausible production. When $α^+ → 0$ the surplus is worthless, so $q → 1/α^-$ increases: the producer bids more aggressively to sell its energy on the day-ahead market, as long as the deficit penalty stays moderate.

Part IV: The value of the stochastic solution

We compare the following quantities, all evaluated on a large test set:

  • WS (wait-and-see): the revenue with perfect information, $\mathbb{E}[∑_t λ_t w_t]$.

  • RP (recourse problem): the revenue of the two-stage solution.

  • EEV: the revenue of the bid $\mathbb{E}[w_t]$ (the solution of the deterministic problem on the mean scenario).

The expected value of perfect information is $\text{EVPI} = \text{WS} - \text{RP}$ and the value of the stochastic solution is $\text{VSS} = \text{RP} - \text{EEV}$.

Question 11. Compute WS, RP, EEV, EVPI and VSS. Why do we evaluate them on new scenarios, not on the scenarios used to compute $x^\star$? Show that $\text{EEV} ≤ \text{RP} ≤ \text{WS}$.

Solution. The SAA value is biased upward: $x^\star$ is optimized on the training scenarios, so its in-sample value is too optimistic. Evaluating on independent scenarios removes the bias. The inequalities hold because the mean-value bid is feasible for the two-stage problem, and the two-stage problem is a restriction of the wait-and-see problem ($x$ cannot depend on $w$).

x_ev = vec(mean(wind_train, dims=2))
WS = mean(ws_revenue)
RP = mean(evaluate_bid(x_saa, λ, λp, λm, wind_test))
EEV = mean(evaluate_bid(x_ev, λ, λp, λm, wind_test))

For a single training sample the in-sample value is very noisy: it can be above or below the true value. On average, it overestimates the true optimal value (optimization bias), and the gap closes as $N$ grows. The out-of-sample value is always below the true optimal value. It increases with $N$ and converges to it. The two curves give a statistical confidence interval on the optimal value.

Question 13. Plot the VSS as a function of the deficit ratio $α^- ∈ [1, 3]$, and as a function of the forecast error $σ ∈ [0.1, 1.5]$. When is stochastic optimization worth the effort?

Solution.

function vss(; α_minus=1.3, σ=0.7, N=500)
    lp, lm = imbalance_prices(λ; α_plus=0.8, α_minus=α_minus)
    w_tr = generate_wind_scenarios(farm, v_forecast, N; σ=σ, seed=1)
    w_te = generate_wind_scenarios(farm, v_forecast, 10_000; σ=σ, seed=42)
    rp = mean(evaluate_bid(newsvendor_bid(λ, lp, lm, w_tr), λ, lp, lm, w_te))
    eev = mean(evaluate_bid(vec(mean(w_tr, dims=2)), λ, lp, lm, w_te))
    return rp - eev
end

The VSS is close to zero for $α^- = 1.2$, where $q = 1/2$: the optimal bid is the median, which is close to the mean. It grows with the asymmetry of the penalties, in both directions (for $α^- = 1$, $q = 1$ and the optimal bid is the maximal production). It also grows with the forecast error. With a perfect forecast ($σ → 0$) the problem is deterministic and VSS = 0.

Part V: Going further

Coupling with a battery

We add the battery of the previous tutorial next to the wind farm. The battery can only be charged with wind energy. In each scenario $k$, the energy delivered to the grid is $y_{t,k} - b_{t,k} + p_{t,k}$, and the state of charge follows the dynamics of the battery.

Question 14. Write the two-stage program where the battery decisions $(b, p, s)$ are second-stage variables. Is the problem still separable by hour? Is there still an explicit solution?

Solution.

struct BatteryData
    C::Float64
    P::Float64
    c::Float64
    eta_p::Float64
    eta_d::Float64
end

function build_two_stage_battery_model(farm::WindFarm, bat::BatteryData, λ, λp, λm, wind::Matrix)
    T, N = size(wind)
    model = build_two_stage_model(farm, λ, λp, λm, wind)
    x, y, Δp, Δm = model[:x], model[:y], model[:Δp], model[:Δm]
    @variable(model, 0.0 <= s[1:T+1, 1:N] <= bat.C)
    @variable(model, 0.0 <= b[1:T, 1:N] <= bat.P)
    @variable(model, 0.0 <= p[1:T, 1:N] <= bat.P)
    @constraint(model, [k=1:N], s[1, k] == 0.0)
    @constraint(model, [t=1:T, k=1:N], s[t+1, k] == s[t, k] - p[t, k] / bat.eta_p + bat.eta_d * b[t, k])
    @constraint(model, [t=1:T, k=1:N], b[t, k] <= y[t, k])
    # Replace the balance equation: the delivered energy includes the battery
    delete.(model, model[:balance])
    unregister(model, :balance)
    @constraint(model, balance[t=1:T, k=1:N], y[t, k] - b[t, k] + p[t, k] - x[t] == Δp[t, k] - Δm[t, k])
    return model
end

bat = BatteryData(5.0, 1.0, 0.0, 0.9, 0.9)
bat_model = build_two_stage_battery_model(farm, bat, λ, λp, λm, wind_train)
JuMP.set_optimizer(bat_model, HiGHS.Optimizer)
JuMP.set_silent(bat_model)
JuMP.optimize!(bat_model)

The state of charge couples the hours together: the problem is no longer separable and we have to solve the full LP.

Question 15. In the previous model, the battery decisions at hour $t$ depend on the whole scenario $w^k_1, ⋯, w^k_T$, including the future wind. Is this realistic? Explain why the optimal value of the two-stage problem is an upper bound on the revenue of any implementable strategy. How could we fix the model?

Solution. The battery is operated in real time: at hour $t$ the operator only knows $w_1, ⋯, w_t$. The two-stage model violates this non-anticipativity constraint. It relaxes the true problem and overestimates the revenue. Two remedies are possible: (i) a multistage stochastic program on a scenario tree, with non-anticipativity constraints, or (ii) keep the two-stage bid $x^\star$ and operate the battery with a non-anticipative policy (rolling horizon, or the SDP policy of the battery tutorial). Evaluating (ii) out-of-sample gives a lower bound on the optimal revenue.

Other extensions

  • Uncertain day-ahead price. Sample $λ$ jointly with the wind using the Markov chain of the battery tutorial. Is the problem still separable? What is the optimal bid when $λ$ and $w$ are independent?

  • Risk aversion. Maximize $\mathbb{E}[R] - β \, \text{CVaR}_{0.95}[-R]$ using the Rockafellar–Uryasev reformulation, and plot the expected revenue against the CVaR when $β$ varies.

  • Decomposition. Solve the battery model with the L-shaped (Benders) method, using one subproblem per scenario.
