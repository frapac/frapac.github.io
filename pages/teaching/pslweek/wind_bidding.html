Day-ahead bidding of a wind farm

François Pacaud
November 2026

Motivation

This project is a sequel to the tutorial on the optimal management of an energy storage. It uses the same tools: the language Julia, the modeler JuMP and the solver HiGHS.

] add Plots, JuMP, HiGHS

A wind producer sells its energy on the day-ahead market. Every day before noon, it must commit to the quantity of energy it will deliver during each hour of the following day. At that time, the wind production is not known: it is only forecast. The next day, any deviation between the committed quantity and the actual production is settled on the balancing market at an unfavorable price:

  • If the producer delivers more than committed (surplus), the extra energy is paid less than the day-ahead price.

  • If the producer delivers less than committed (deficit), the missing energy is charged more than the day-ahead price.

This is a two-stage decision problem:

  1. First stage (here-and-now): choose the day-ahead bids, before the wind is known.

  2. Second stage (wait-and-see): observe the wind and settle the imbalances.

The forecaster provides a forecast $\overline{v}_t$ of the wind speed. The realized speed is $v_t = \overline{v}_t + ε_t$, where the forecast error $ε_t$ follows an auto-regressive process:

\[ ε_t = ρ \, ε_{t-1} + σ \, η_t \; , \quad η_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \; , \quad ε_0 = 0 \; . \]

The realized production is $w_t = φ(v_t)$, with $φ$ the power curve. We sample scenarios of the wind production with the function generate_wind_scenarios:

v_forecast = wind_speed_forecast(T)

Part I: Deterministic bidding

We denote by $x_t ∈ [0, \overline{W}]$ the energy bid on the day-ahead market for hour $t$. Given a realization $w_t$ of the wind, the producer delivers $y_t ≤ w_t$ ($w_t - y_t$ is curtailed) and the revenue for hour $t$ is

\[ λ_t x_t + λ^+_t \, (y_t - x_t)^+ - λ^-_t \, (x_t - y_t)^+ \; . \]

As the prices are positive, it is never profitable to curtail: we can set $y_t = w_t$. The following function evaluates the revenue of a bid $x$ on a set of scenarios:

"""
    evaluate_bid(x, λ, λp, λm, wind)

Return the revenue [€] of the bid `x` on each scenario of `wind`
(matrix of dimension `(T, n_scenarios)`).
"""
function evaluate_bid(x::Vector, λ::Vector, λp::Vector, λm::Vector, wind::Matrix)
    T, nscen = size(wind)
    revenue = zeros(nscen)
    for k in 1:nscen, t in 1:T
        surplus = max(wind[t, k] - x[t], 0.0)
        deficit = max(x[t] - wind[t, k], 0.0)
        revenue[k] += λ[t] * x[t] + λp[t] * surplus - λm[t] * deficit
    end
    return revenue
end

Part II: Two-stage stochastic program

We approximate the uncertainty by $N$ scenarios $w^1, ⋯, w^N$, each with probability $1/N$ (Sample Average Approximation, SAA). We introduce the second-stage variables

  • $Δ^+_{t,k} ≥ 0$: surplus at hour $t$ in scenario $k$ [MWh]

  • $Δ^-_{t,k} ≥ 0$: deficit at hour $t$ in scenario $k$ [MWh]

  • $0 ≤ y_{t,k} ≤ w^k_t$: delivered energy at hour $t$ in scenario $k$ [MWh]

The two-stage stochastic program writes

\[ \begin{aligned} \max_{x, Δ^+, Δ^-, y} \; & ∑_{t=1}^T λ_t x_t + \frac{1}{N} ∑_{k=1}^N ∑_{t=1}^T \Big( λ^+_t Δ^+_{t,k} - λ^-_t Δ^-_{t,k} \Big) \\ \text{s.t.}\quad & y_{t,k} - x_t = Δ^+_{t,k} - Δ^-_{t,k} \\ & 0 ≤ x_t ≤ \overline{W} \; , \quad 0 ≤ y_{t,k} ≤ w^k_t \; , \quad Δ^+_{t,k}, Δ^-_{t,k} ≥ 0 \end{aligned} \]

We implement the problem with JuMP:

using JuMP, HiGHS

"""
    build_two_stage_model(farm, λ, λp, λm, wind)

Build the SAA of the two-stage bidding problem on the scenarios `wind`
(matrix of dimension `(T, n_scenarios)`).
"""
function build_two_stage_model(farm::WindFarm, λ, λp, λm, wind::Matrix)
    T, N = size(wind)
    model = Model()
    # First stage
    @variable(model, 0.0 <= x[1:T] <= farm.Wmax)
    # Second stage
    @variable(model, 0.0 <= y[t=1:T, k=1:N] <= wind[t, k])
    @variable(model, Δp[1:T, 1:N] >= 0.0)
    @variable(model, Δm[1:T, 1:N] >= 0.0)
    @constraint(model, balance[t=1:T, k=1:N], y[t, k] - x[t] == Δp[t, k] - Δm[t, k])
    @objective(
        model,
        Max,
        sum(λ[t] * x[t] for t in 1:T) +
        sum(λp[t] * Δp[t, k] - λm[t] * Δm[t, k] for t in 1:T, k in 1:N) / N
    )
    return model
end

Part III: Explicit solution

Without storage, the objective and the constraints separate hour by hour. For each hour $t$ we have to solve a one-dimensional problem:

\[ \max_{0 ≤ x_t ≤ \overline{W}} \; λ_t x_t + \mathbb{E}\Big[ λ^+_t (w_t - x_t)^+ - λ^-_t (x_t - w_t)^+ \Big] \]

This is the well-known newsvendor problem.

Part IV: The value of the stochastic solution

We compare the following quantities, all evaluated on a large test set:

  • WS (wait-and-see): the revenue with perfect information, $\mathbb{E}[∑_t λ_t w_t]$.

  • RP (recourse problem): the revenue of the two-stage solution.

  • EEV: the revenue of the bid $\mathbb{E}[w_t]$ (the solution of the deterministic problem on the mean scenario).

The expected value of perfect information is $\text{EVPI} = \text{WS} - \text{RP}$ and the value of the stochastic solution is $\text{VSS} = \text{RP} - \text{EEV}$.
